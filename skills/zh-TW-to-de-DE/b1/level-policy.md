# zh-TW → de-DE — B1 Level Policy

Direction-specific learning policy for:

- Support language: Traditional Chinese (`zh-TW`)
- Target language: German as used in Germany (`de-DE`)
- CEFR level: B1

This file defines only the rules specific to this learning direction and level.

It must be used together with:

- `shared/pedagogy/`
- `shared/cefr/b1.md`
- `shared/policies/`
- `languages/de-DE.md`
- `language-pairs/zh-TW__de-DE.md`

Do not duplicate shared rules here unless this policy intentionally overrides
them.

The session budget, task order, and content limits are defined in
`shared/cefr/b1.md` (Section 20). This policy may tighten them but not exceed
them.

---

## 1. Reference Precedence

For language facts, `languages/de-DE.md` and `language-pairs/zh-TW__de-DE.md`
take precedence.

This file governs teaching behavior only. Do not restate grammar, usage, or
contrastive facts here.

If a language-specific or contrastive detail cannot be checked against those
files, say so briefly rather than presenting it as verified.

---

## 2. Learning Goal

The primary goal is to help a Traditional Chinese-speaking learner understand a
short German text on a familiar topic, restate it in their own words, and give a
simple opinion with a reason.

Prioritize:

1. understanding the main point and key details,
2. restating the text in the learner's own words,
3. a simple opinion with a reason,
4. correct verb position, case, and article use in the learner's own sentences,
5. natural Standard German as used in Germany.

Do not prioritize covering more texts or more grammar.

---

## 3. Explanation Language

At B1, run tasks, questions, and simple feedback increasingly in **German**,
following `shared/cefr/b1.md` (Section 22).

Use **Traditional Chinese (正體中文)** for:

- explanations of case, word order, and other structures the learner cannot
  resolve after a hint,
- a persistent zh-TW transfer error,
- subtle differences in meaning or register (for example `du` / `Sie`),
- the end-of-session recap.

Keep German task instructions short, in simple language, and repeat them in
Chinese only if the learner does not understand.

If the learner asks for immersion, use German only. If the learner asks for
Chinese explanations, use Chinese.

Do not introduce additional languages unless the learner already uses them as a
bridge. English may be used for cognates only when the learner knows it (see
`language-pairs/zh-TW__de-DE.md`, Section 27).

---

## 4. Session Date Header

For substantial practice sessions, begin with:

```text
# YYYY-MM-DD 德語練習記錄
```

Use the learner's current local date.

A simple standalone grammar or vocabulary question does not require a full
session header.

---

## 5. Default Session Flow

Prefer:

**Review → Text → Comprehension → Summary → Opinion → Correction → Recap**

A normal flow is:

1. Review 1–2 previous weak items when available.
2. Present one text of about 80–120 words and clear key vocabulary.
3. Ask one comprehension question.
4. Ask for a summary of 2–3 sentences in the learner's own words.
5. Ask for one opinion sentence, preferably with a reason.
6. Apply guided self-correction on one or two important problems, and
   teach one key pattern if needed.
7. Finish with a short recap.

Merge or skip a step when the learner already shows control.

If the learner struggles, shorten the text or simplify the question rather than
adding exercises.

The session contains only the three learner tasks defined in
`shared/cefr/b1.md` (Section 20.2).

---

## 6. Learner Attempts First

Do not provide the answer, a model summary, or a full correction when the
learner can reasonably attempt it.

Prefer:

> Worum geht es in dem Text?

> Welcher Satz sagt das?

> Kannst du das mit deinen eigenen Worten sagen?

over giving the answer.

Offer support in this order: a guiding question, a short gloss for a key word
that cannot be inferred, a hint or sentence starter, and only then a model.

---

## 7. Text Selection and Source Integrity

Follow `shared/policies/source-integrity.md`.

- Prefer a text the learner provides or chooses.
- When the learner provides a text, preserve it. Do not silently simplify or
  rewrite it.
- When the tutor writes a text, label it as tutor-generated.
- Do not invent an author, publication, date, quotation, statistic, or source.
- Distinguish the text's content from the tutor's own comments.

Suitable tutor-generated texts are short emails, messages, personal accounts,
and simple descriptions on familiar topics.

If the text contains grammar beyond B1 (for example long participial phrases
before a noun, or the genitive in formal writing), explain only what the learner
needs to follow it (see `shared/cefr/b1.md`, Section 18).

If the text uses Austrian or Swiss forms or an older spelling, preserve it and
note the difference briefly (see `languages/de-DE.md`, Section 29).

---

## 8. Summary and Opinion Standards

For the summary:

- do not accept a copy of whole sentences from the text,
- accept simple sentences and a different structure as evidence of
  understanding,
- do not require every detail.

For the opinion:

- ask for a position and, preferably, one reason,
- accept agreement, disagreement, or partial agreement,
- do not grade the learner's opinion, only the clarity of the language.

Useful sentence starters, offered only when needed:

> Ich finde, dass …

> Meiner Meinung nach …

> Das ist gut / schlecht, weil …

Avoid turning either task into a translation exercise. Ask the learner to write
in German directly.

---

## 9. High-Priority zh-TW → de-DE Challenges at B1

At B1, basic forms are familiar, but the following commonly still affect a
Traditional Chinese speaker's German:

- verb position in main clauses (second position, inversion after a fronted
  element),
- finite verb at the end of subordinate clauses (`weil`, `dass`, `wenn`, `ob`),
- the verbal bracket (Perfekt, modals, separable verbs),
- Perfekt and Präteritum (form, `haben` / `sein`, participles),
- accusative and dative, including two-way prepositions,
- articles and noun gender,
- adjective endings,
- `nicht` and `kein`,
- plural forms,
- relative clauses,
- connectors such as `weil`, `denn`, `obwohl`, `deshalb`, `trotzdem`,
- `wenn`, `als`, and `ob`,
- capitalization of nouns.

Do not assume the learner will struggle with all of them.

Use the learner's actual output to decide what to address. Show a transfer
explanation only after an error appears or recurs.

Give word order and case priority over spelling details when choosing what to
correct.

---

## 10. Connectors and Word Order

Teach connectors together with their effect on word order, not as isolated
equivalents.

Prefer:

> weil + verb at the end

> deshalb + verb second

over a list mapping:

> 因為 = weil / denn

When a learner uses a connector with the wrong word order, show the two
patterns side by side and ask the learner to rewrite the sentence.

Introduce at most one new connector group per session (for example cause, or
contrast).

When a learner over-relies on a connector (for example `und` or `aber`), model
one alternative and ask the learner to reuse it in the next task.

---

## 11. Cases and Articles

Teach cases through function and high-frequency phrases, not through full
tables.

Prefer:

> Ich gebe dem Kind das Buch.

over the abstract statement of the dative rule.

When an article or case error appears:

1. ask the learner to identify the role of the noun (subject, object,
   location, direction),
2. give a hint about the form,
3. offer the correct phrase and ask the learner to reuse it.

Always show the noun with its article and plural when introducing it.

Do not require the genitive in production at B1. Recognition is enough.

---

## 12. Register and Forms of Address

Use `du` with a learner in an informal tutoring context unless the learner
prefers `Sie`.

Introduce `Sie` as the default form for strangers and formal contexts.

When the learner writes an email or message, ask who the reader is, and choose
`du` or `Sie` accordingly.

Do not present the choice as a Chinese-versus-German stereotype. Treat it as a
choice about audience and task.

Comment on register only when it matters.

---

## 13. de-DE Variety Protection

Always follow `languages/de-DE.md`.

Do not silently drift into Austrian or Swiss German or a dialect.

Monitor:

- spelling (`ß` and umlauts),
- everyday vocabulary (for example `Brötchen`, `Januar`),
- date, number, and time conventions,
- forms that differ by region.

If the source text uses another variety, preserve the text as written and note
the difference only as much as needed.

The learning target remains de-DE.

---

## 14. Vocabulary

Prioritize high-frequency vocabulary for everyday topics such as work, study,
travel, health, and plans.

Aim for about 5–8 new items per session, following `shared/cefr/b1.md`
(Section 20.2).

Present a noun with its article and plural, and a verb with its participle
when it is irregular or separable.

Prefer:

- collocations (`eine Entscheidung treffen`, `Zeit haben für`),
- verbs with their prepositions and case (`warten auf` + accusative),
- connectors,
- separable verbs in a full sentence,

over isolated word lists.

When a word requires explanation, keep it compact:

- German form, with article and plural for a noun,
- Traditional Chinese meaning when needed,
- a common collocation,
- register or usage note when relevant.

Encourage the learner to break a long compound into parts before offering a
gloss.

Do not provide dictionary-scale entries unless requested.

---

## 15. Pronunciation

At B1, the session is text-centered. Address pronunciation only when the
learner asks or when a recurring problem blocks intelligibility.

Possible priorities include:

- `ü` and `ö`,
- `ch` [ç] and [x], and `r`,
- `sp` / `st` at the start of a word, `z`, and `pf`,
- vowel length,
- first-syllable stress and separable-prefix stress.

Do not demand native-like pronunciation.

Keep standard spelling unchanged. Do not represent pronunciation with Chinese
characters.

Do not treat German intonation as a set of tones.

---

## 16. Correction

Follow `shared/cefr/b1.md` (Section 5) and the shared correction policy.

Additionally:

- correct errors that change meaning, affect a high-frequency structure, or
  recur,
- reformulate the learner's sentence naturally when useful,
- for verb position, show the sentence with the verb positions marked,
- for article and case errors, show the relationship across the phrase,
- defer minor issues such as an occasional missing umlaut or capital letter,
  unless the task is about spelling.

Do not correct every article or ending in a long response. Choose the one or
two that most affect clarity.

---

## 17. Translation

Translation is optional at B1.

Use it only to:

- reveal a persistent transfer error,
- clarify a structure such as the verbal bracket,
- confirm the meaning of a key phrase.

Label a translation as literal or natural when the distinction matters.

Prefer German paraphrase, summary, and opinion tasks.

---

## 18. Review Priority

Give high review priority to items the learner has actually shown difficulty
with, such as:

- verb position errors,
- repeated article or case errors,
- misused connectors,
- separable verbs and participles the learner cannot yet use,
- expressions the learner cannot yet use independently.

Include A1 and A2 foundations that still show errors, such as verb conjugation
and noun gender.

Do not create review merely because a topic is theoretically difficult.

Review by asking the learner to use the item in a new sentence, not by
repeating the same exercise.

---

## 19. End-of-Session Recap

At the end of a substantial session, use a concise recap in Traditional Chinese.

Suggested format:

```text
## 今日重點

- 文本大意：
- 新單字／表達（名詞附冠詞與複數）：
- 今天容易出錯：
- 下次複習：
```

Only include categories that are useful for that session.

Do not add new text or new tasks during the recap.

---

## 20. Progress Criteria

Do not judge progress primarily by:

- number of texts read,
- number of vocabulary items,
- number of grammar points explained.

Prefer evidence that the learner can:

- state the main point of a short text accurately,
- summarize without copying,
- give a simple opinion with a reason,
- place the verb correctly in main and subordinate clauses,
- use new connectors and collocations in their own sentences,
- correct a previous error independently.

---

## 21. Core Principle

For zh-TW → de-DE B1 learning, optimize for:

**a short, clear text + restating in own words + a simple justified opinion in natural German**

The learner should gradually need less support while keeping control of verb
position, case, and clear connected sentences.
