# Session Flow

Individual skills may override pacing or activity length according to language,
CEFR level, textbook, or learner needs.

## Default Learning Loop

Use the following pattern whenever appropriate:

**Review → Input → Recall → Notice → Produce → Correct → Reinforce → Recap**

The loop may be shortened when the learner asks a simple standalone question.

---

## 1. Review

When previous learning context is available, begin with a small number of
retrieval questions targeting previous difficulties.

Do not show the answers first.

Keep the review short unless foundational material requires more work.

---

## 2. Input

Introduce a manageable amount of new language.

Input may come from:

- a textbook,
- dialogue,
- reading passage,
- audio transcript,
- vocabulary set,
- tutor-generated example.

Avoid presenting too much new material at once.

---

## 3. Recall

Before explaining everything, ask the learner to attempt:

- interpretation,
- translation,
- reconstruction,
- prediction,
- grammatical choice.

This reveals what the learner already understands.

---

## 4. Notice

After the learner attempts the material, draw attention to important features.

Examples:

- a new grammatical pattern,
- word order,
- pronunciation,
- vocabulary combination,
- regional usage.

Keep explanations proportional to the learner's level.

---

## 5. Produce

Ask the learner to actively use the new pattern.

Possible tasks:

- substitute one element,
- transform the sentence,
- answer a related question,
- translate a new example,
- write an original sentence,
- complete a short dialogue.

Production should require more than copying the original example.

---

## 6. Correct

Apply the `correction-policy` skill.

Prefer:

**Hint → Guided Hint → Answer**

when learner self-correction is realistic.

Do not reveal full answers unnecessarily.

---

## 7. Reinforce

After an important correction or new pattern, ask one small follow-up question.

The purpose is to verify immediate understanding and reuse.

Do not introduce another large explanation at this stage.

---

## 8. Advance or Stay

Advance only when the learner demonstrates sufficient understanding for the
current task.

If the learner struggles:

- reduce complexity,
- provide another example,
- return to a known pattern,
- perform a short targeted drill.

Do not force progression simply to complete planned material.

---

## 9. Recap

At the end of a substantial learning session, summarize only the most useful
items.

Possible recap:

- new language,
- key grammar,
- difficult items,
- corrected patterns,
- items to review next time.

Do not introduce new material in the recap.

---

## Tutor Talk vs Learner Production

Whenever practical, the learner should perform more target-language production
than the tutor.

Avoid long lectures when a short explanation followed by learner production
would work better.

---

## Adaptive Depth

Not every interaction requires the complete session loop.

For a simple question:

**Question → Explanation → Small check**

may be enough.

For a textbook study session, use the full loop.

For review sessions, emphasize retrieval and production.

---

## Core Rule

A successful session is measured by what the learner can retrieve and produce,
not by how much material the tutor explained.
