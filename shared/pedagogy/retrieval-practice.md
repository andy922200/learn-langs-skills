# Retrieval Practice

## Core Principle

Prefer:

**Retrieve → Check → Correct → Reuse**

over:

**Read → Recognize → Move on**

Recognition is easier than production and should not be treated as evidence of
mastery.

---

## Retrieval Before Explanation

Whenever the learner may already possess enough knowledge to attempt an answer,
ask first.

Examples:

Instead of:

> 這句的意思是……

prefer:

> 你覺得這句是什麼意思？

Instead of:

> 正確冠詞是……

prefer:

> 這裡你會選哪個冠詞？

Instead of immediately translating vocabulary:

> 你還記得這個詞嗎？

---

## Useful Retrieval Tasks

Possible task types include:

- translate from the learner's support language,
- reconstruct a sentence from keywords,
- complete a missing word,
- choose between two forms,
- correct an incorrect sentence,
- conjugate a known verb,
- recall vocabulary from a definition,
- answer a question using a previously learned pattern,
- transform an example into a new context.

Prefer short retrieval tasks over repeated rereading.

---

## Production Over Recognition

Whenever appropriate, move from recognition to production.

A useful progression is:

1. recognize,
2. recall with support,
3. produce with a model,
4. produce with reduced support,
5. produce independently.

Do not remain indefinitely at multiple-choice or recognition-level tasks.

---

## Do Not Reveal Too Early

If the learner hesitates, do not immediately reveal the answer.

Use progressive hints.

However, avoid turning retrieval into guessing.

If the learner clearly does not know the material, teach or review it before
asking again.

---

## Variation

Do not always repeat the exact same question.

Reuse the underlying pattern in a slightly different context.

Example:

Previously learned:

> I live in Lisbon.

Later retrieval might ask:

> Say that Maria lives in Porto.

The goal is to retrieve the structure, not memorize one fixed sentence.

---

## Retrieval Difficulty

Retrieval should be challenging enough to require recall but not so difficult
that the learner is repeatedly unable to respond.

Adjust:

- vocabulary,
- sentence length,
- number of missing elements,
- amount of prompting,
- time since previous exposure.

---

## Core Rule

If the learner can retrieve and reuse a pattern without seeing the answer first,
that is stronger evidence of learning than simply recognizing it.
