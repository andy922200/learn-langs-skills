# Spaced Review

## Core Principle

Prefer:

**Learn → Delay → Retrieve → Reuse**

over:

**Learn → Repeat immediately many times → Forget**

Review should prioritize material that has demonstrated weakness.

---

## What to Review

Give higher priority to items that the learner:

- answered incorrectly,
- needed multiple hints for,
- repeatedly confused,
- recognized but could not produce,
- previously corrected but later missed again.

Give lower priority to items that have been produced correctly several times
without assistance.

---

## Review Queue

When previous learning context is available, maintain a conceptual review queue.

Useful states include:

- new,
- weak,
- improving,
- stable.

The exact labels do not need to be shown to the learner.

They exist to guide exercise selection.

---

## Review Timing

Prefer reviewing difficult material:

1. later in the same session,
2. at the beginning of a future session,
3. again after further delay if it remains difficult.

Do not rely only on immediate repetition.

---

## Modified Retrieval

Avoid repeating the exact same prompt every time.

Change:

- person,
- number,
- vocabulary,
- sentence context,
- translation direction,
- question type.

Example:

Original:

> My name is Ana.

Later review:

> How would Pedro introduce himself?

The underlying structure remains the same while the surface form changes.

---

## Review Before New Material

When previous session context is available, begin with a small number of review
items before introducing new material.

Typically use only a few targeted questions.

Review should not consume the entire session unless the learner is struggling
with foundational material.

---

## Mastery Signal

An item can be treated as increasingly stable when the learner can:

- recall it after a delay,
- use it without hints,
- apply it in a changed context,
- distinguish it from a commonly confused alternative.

One correct answer immediately after seeing the explanation is not sufficient
evidence of mastery.

---

## Core Rule

Review should be driven by demonstrated learner difficulty rather than by equal
repetition of everything.
