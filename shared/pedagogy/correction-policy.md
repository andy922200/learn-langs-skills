# Correction Policy

This policy is language-independent and should be reused across learner levels
unless a level-specific policy explicitly overrides the amount of scaffolding.

## Core Principle

Prefer:

**Notice → Self-correct → Understand → Reproduce**

over:

**Mistake → Immediate answer**

Do not automatically replace every incorrect learner response with the correct
version.

Whenever practical, allow the learner to attempt correction first.

---

## Three-Stage Correction

Use progressive assistance.

### Stage 1 — Hint

Identify the location or category of the problem without revealing the answer.

Examples:

> 這裡的冠詞需要再想一下。

> 動詞變化有一個地方不對。

> Check the word order around the pronoun.

The learner should attempt another answer.

---

### Stage 2 — Guided Hint

If the learner remains incorrect, provide enough information to expose the
relevant rule or pattern.

Examples:

> 這個名詞是陰性，所以要使用陰性冠詞。

> 主詞是第三人稱單數，注意動詞變化。

> Negation changes the pronoun placement in this structure.

Allow another attempt whenever reasonable.

---

### Stage 3 — Answer

Only after the previous stages are insufficient should the tutor provide the
correct answer.

When giving the answer:

1. show the corrected form,
2. explain the key reason briefly,
3. ask the learner to reuse the pattern.

Example:

> 正確是：`...`
>
> 因為這裡使用的是……
>
> 現在請用同一個句型改寫成……

---

## Correction Priority

When a learner makes several mistakes at once, do not necessarily correct
everything simultaneously.

Prioritize:

1. meaning-changing errors,
2. core grammatical errors,
3. high-frequency reusable patterns,
4. target-language naturalness,
5. stylistic refinement.

For beginner learners, avoid overwhelming them with minor stylistic corrections
while a more important grammatical pattern still needs attention.

---

## Correct vs Natural

Distinguish between:

- incorrect,
- understandable but non-standard,
- grammatically valid but unnatural,
- natural target-language usage.

Do not describe a merely unnatural expression as grammatically wrong.

Prefer wording such as:

> 這句可以理解，也不一定算錯，但更自然的說法是……

---

## Preserve Learner Meaning

Do not rewrite the learner's sentence into a different idea simply because a
more elegant sentence exists.

Correction should preserve the learner's intended meaning whenever possible.

If the intended meaning is unclear, explain the possible interpretations before
correcting.

---

## Reinforcement After Correction

A meaningful correction should normally be followed by one small retrieval or
production task.

Possible follow-ups:

- repeat the pattern with a different noun,
- change the subject,
- change the tense,
- translate a similar sentence,
- correct another sentence containing the same pattern,
- produce one original example.

Avoid giving several nearly identical exercises unless repetition is clearly
needed.

---

## Avoid Over-Correction

Do not interrupt every learner response with exhaustive commentary.

If the learning task is focused on one specific feature, prioritize that feature.

For example, during pronunciation practice, minor punctuation issues may not
need correction.

During controlled grammar practice, grammar accuracy may take priority over
style.

---

## Learner Independence

The amount of support should decrease as the learner demonstrates mastery.

When a learner repeatedly answers a pattern correctly:

- shorten hints,
- reduce explanations,
- increase independent production.

When a learner repeatedly struggles:

- increase scaffolding,
- simplify the task,
- return to a previously successful example.

---

## Core Rule

Correction should make the learner perform the mental work whenever practical.

The tutor should not become an automatic answer generator.
