# CEFR B2 Learning Policy

Shared level policy for CEFR B2 language-learning skills.

This document is language-independent.

It defines the expected difficulty, scaffolding, task size, and learner output
for a B2 learner.

Language-specific grammar belongs in `languages/`.

Learner-language transfer and contrastive explanations belong in
`language-pairs/`.

General teaching behavior belongs in `shared/pedagogy/`.

---

## 1. Core Goal

At B2, prioritize the ability to understand the main ideas of complex texts and
to express a reasoned viewpoint clearly.

The learner should gradually become able to:

- identify an author's claim and stance,
- distinguish the main argument from supporting detail,
- restate ideas in their own words,
- respond to a text with a structured, reasoned view.

Prefer:

**demanding but manageable text + thinking task + own-words output**

over:

**sentence-level grammar drills + passive comprehension**

B2 extends B1 rather than replacing it. Recurring errors from earlier levels
may still return in review, but they should be handled inside meaningful tasks,
not as isolated drills.

---

## 2. Input Difficulty

Input should normally be:

- a connected text with a clear line of argument,
- about a topic that can be understood without specialist knowledge,
- clearly organized, with some implicit meaning,
- containing a mix of familiar and less familiar vocabulary,
- containing some new words that can be inferred from context.

Prefer:

- opinion pieces and columns,
- feature and news articles,
- essays and reviews,
- longer emails and letters,
- authentic or lightly adapted textbook passages.

Avoid highly specialized, dense, or heavily idiomatic text unless the learner
has chosen it.

---

## 3. Amount of New Material

Introduce new material in small units.

As a general principle:

- one text per session,
- at most one language focus, and only when the learner's own output shows a
  need,
- a limited number of new lexical items,
- frequent reuse of new expressions in the learner's own output.

Do not treat text length or content volume as a learning target.

If the learner struggles, shorten the text or narrow the task rather than
adding support material.

---

## 4. Scaffolding

B2 learners should receive minimal scaffolding.

Useful support includes:

- a guiding question,
- a short gloss for a key word that cannot be inferred,
- a useful connector or discourse marker on request,
- a brief hint before any full answer.

Support should be offered when needed, not by default.

Prefer:

**minimal scaffolding + learner-generated analysis**

over:

**complete answer + passive reading**

---

## 5. Correction

Use the shared correction policy.

At B2:

- let the learner attempt self-correction first,
- focus on one or two important problems at a time,
- prioritize errors that change meaning, weaken the argument, or recur,
- reformulate the learner's sentence naturally when useful,
- comment on clarity, precision, and register when they matter,
- avoid correcting every stylistic imperfection.

If the learner clearly lacks the underlying knowledge, teach the pattern
briefly before asking for another attempt.

---

## 6. Grammar Depth

Grammar explanations should be brief and tied to the learner's output.

Reduce sentence-by-sentence grammar drills. Address grammar when it:

- distorts the learner's meaning,
- appears repeatedly in the learner's own writing,
- is needed to understand how the text builds its argument.

At B2, the tutor may:

- explain differences in nuance, register, or structure,
- compare near-equivalent forms,
- use standard grammatical terminology.

Avoid long theoretical explanations unless explicitly requested.

---

## 7. Production

B2 production is built around the text the learner has just read.

Useful production tasks include:

- identifying the author's main claim,
- paraphrasing a passage or argument in own words,
- responding to a text with a reasoned view,
- agreeing or disagreeing with support,
- comparing the text with personal experience or another viewpoint,
- rewriting a passage in a different register.

Production should be open but focused, with a clear task and a clear audience.

---

## 8. Production Length

Keep expected output focused.

Typical B2 production may include:

- one or two sentences stating the author's claim,
- a paraphrase of a short passage or of the whole argument,
- a response of three to five connected sentences.

Do not require long essays as the default task.

If longer writing is requested, build gradually from a structured short
response toward a paragraph.

---

## 9. Reading

B2 reading material should normally:

- be about 120–180 words per session,
- have a discernible argument or point of view,
- deal with topics beyond immediate personal experience,
- contain some less familiar grammar and vocabulary,
- allow the main point to be understood without every word.

Useful materials include:

- opinion pieces and columns,
- news and feature articles,
- essays and reviews,
- longer emails and reports,
- short literary or narrative extracts.

Encourage the learner to infer unknown words from context and to notice how
the author signals the argument before offering a gloss.

Do not require understanding every word before progressing.

---

## 10. Listening

When listening material is used, prioritize:

- short passages with a clear point of view,
- natural speech at a moderate pace,
- main-argument understanding before detail.

Allow replay or segmentation when possible.

Use the same task pattern as reading: identify the speaker's claim, paraphrase,
then respond.

---

## 11. Vocabulary

Prioritize vocabulary that helps the learner discuss ideas and build arguments.

Common domains include:

- work, education, and society,
- media, technology, and the environment,
- health and lifestyle,
- culture and current affairs,
- opinions, evidence, and reasoning.

Prefer teaching vocabulary in collocations, fixed expressions, and discourse
markers rather than isolated word lists.

Useful discourse markers include those for concession, contrast, cause, result,
emphasis, and stating a position.

---

## 12. Retrieval

Use short retrieval when it supports the main task.

Good B2 retrieval tasks include:

- recall the author's main claim without looking at the text,
- restate a supporting point,
- explain a new expression in target-language terms,
- reuse a new expression in a new context.

Avoid excessively difficult recall that turns into guessing.

---

## 13. Review

Previously difficult material should return regularly.

Prioritize material that the learner:

- misinterpreted,
- needed several hints for,
- repeatedly misuses in their own writing,
- cannot yet use independently.

Prefer reviewing an item by asking the learner to use it in a new sentence
rather than by repeating an isolated exercise.

One correct answer immediately after explanation does not indicate mastery.

---

## 14. Learner Independence

Support should gradually decrease.

A useful progression is:

1. guiding question,
2. task prompt only,
3. independent production.

At B2, expect the learner to work at steps 2–3 in most tasks.

When a learner can reliably produce a pattern, stop over-explaining it.

---

## 15. Translation

Translation is rarely needed at B2.

Use it only to:

- reveal a persistent transfer error,
- clarify a subtle difference in meaning,
- compare register or nuance between languages.

Prefer target-language paraphrase, summary, and response tasks.

---

## 16. Open-Ended Tasks

Open-ended tasks should have a clear focus, but need not be step-by-step.

Instead of:

> Write about the article.

prefer:

> 1. State the author's main claim in one sentence.
> 2. Paraphrase the argument in your own words.
> 3. Respond in three to five sentences: do you agree, and why?

The learner may choose their own structure within the task.

---

## 17. Error Tolerance

Clear, well-supported expression takes priority over stylistic perfection.

Do not require native-like language.

Correct errors especially when they:

- change meaning,
- weaken or confuse the argument,
- block comprehension,
- repeatedly occur.

Minor stylistic issues may be deferred.

---

## 18. Advanced Material

Do not proactively introduce advanced grammar simply because it is related to
the current topic.

If source material contains grammar beyond normal B2 scope:

1. explain only what is needed,
2. keep terminology minimal,
3. identify it as advanced when useful,
4. return focus to the B2 learning objective.

Do not redesign authentic source material merely to hide all advanced forms.

---

## 19. Session Pacing

B2 sessions should favor a short, focused cycle.

Prefer:

**read → identify the claim → paraphrase → respond → correct**

rather than long uninterrupted explanation or drills.

The level-wide session budget is defined in Section 20.

A language-specific skill may define stricter pacing for its own materials.

Such material-specific limits belong in the skill or language-specific level
policy, and may tighten but not exceed Section 20.

---

## 20. Session Budget

A default B2 session is designed to last approximately 6 minutes.

Time is a design intention, not a measurement. The tutor cannot reliably track
elapsed time, so enforce the budget by **interaction rounds**.

One round = one tutor prompt + one learner response.

### 20.1 Round Budget

A default session is approximately 5–7 rounds:

- review of previously weak items: ~1 round,
- reading the text: ~1 round,
- identifying the author's claim: ~1 round,
- paraphrase: ~1–2 rounds,
- response: ~1–2 rounds,
- guided correction and concise recap: ~1 round.

These are proportions, not a script. Merge or skip a step when the learner
already shows control.

B2 rounds are fewer than at lower levels because each learner response is
longer and requires more thinking.

### 20.2 Content Budget

Per session:

- 1 text of about 120–180 words,
- 1 claim-identification task,
- 1 paraphrase task,
- 1 response of 3–5 sentences,
- at most 1 language focus, drawn from the learner's own output,
- approximately 5–8 new lexical items.

The session contains only these three learner tasks. Do not add sentence-level
grammar drills.

The three tasks should follow this order:

1. **Claim**: the learner identifies the author's main claim, and where
   relevant the supporting reasons, in one or two sentences.
2. **Paraphrase**: the learner restates the argument or a key passage in their
   own words, without copying whole phrases from the text.
3. **Response**: the learner writes 3–5 connected sentences responding to the
   text, such as agreeing or disagreeing with a reason, or adding a
   counterpoint.

The maximum is a ceiling, not a target.

Do not fill the quota when the learner needs more time. If the learner
struggles, shorten the text or narrow the task rather than adding exercises.

### 20.3 When the Budget Is Reached

Stop introducing new material and finish with reinforcement or a short recap.

If the learner asks to continue, allow review, grammar clarification,
vocabulary practice, rewriting, or error correction, but do not advance to new
content in the same session.

---

## 21. Success Criteria

Do not measure B2 progress mainly by:

- lesson count,
- page count,
- number of rules explained.

Prefer evidence that the learner can:

- identify the main claim and stance of a text,
- separate the argument from supporting detail,
- paraphrase accurately in their own words,
- respond with a clear, reasoned view,
- use connectors and new expressions naturally,
- correct a known error.

---

## 22. Tutor Language

Explanations should use the learner's designated support language when helpful.

At B2, tasks, questions, and feedback should mainly be in the target language.
The support language may be used for subtle explanations of nuance or
difficult grammar.

Do not force all explanations into the target language at B2 unless the learner
requests immersion.

---

## 23. Core Principle

At B2, the tutor should optimize for:

**identifying the argument + paraphrase in own words + reasoned response**

rather than speed, breadth, or grammatical completeness.
