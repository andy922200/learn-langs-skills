# CEFR B1 Learning Policy

Shared level policy for CEFR B1 language-learning skills.

This document is language-independent.

It defines the expected difficulty, scaffolding, task size, and learner output
for a B1 learner.

Language-specific grammar belongs in `languages/`.

Learner-language transfer and contrastive explanations belong in
`language-pairs/`.

General teaching behavior belongs in `shared/pedagogy/`.

---

## 1. Core Goal

At B1, prioritize the ability to understand the main points of clear texts on
familiar topics, and to express experiences, reasons, and opinions in simple
connected language.

The learner should gradually become able to:

- follow the main idea and key details of a short text,
- restate what they read in their own words,
- give a simple opinion and a reason for it.

Prefer:

**short authentic-style text + focused understanding + own-words output**

over:

**long input + passive reading + isolated sentence drills**

B1 extends A2 rather than replacing it. Known A1 and A2 patterns should
continue to be used, and may still return in review when errors persist.

---

## 2. Input Difficulty

Input should normally be:

- a short connected text,
- about a familiar or everyday topic,
- clearly organized,
- mostly high-frequency vocabulary,
- containing some new words that can be inferred from context.

Prefer:

- short articles and blog-style posts,
- emails and messages,
- short narratives and personal accounts,
- simple descriptions and explanations,
- textbook reading passages.

Avoid dense, abstract, or highly idiomatic text.

Avoid several unfamiliar grammar structures in the same passage.

---

## 3. Amount of New Material

Introduce new material in small units.

As a general principle:

- one short text per session,
- one major new language focus at most,
- a limited number of new lexical items,
- frequent reuse of known language in the learner's own output.

Do not treat text length or content volume as a learning target.

If the learner struggles, shorten or simplify the text rather than adding more
support material.

---

## 4. Scaffolding

B1 learners should receive light scaffolding.

Useful support includes:

- a short gloss for a key unknown word,
- a sentence starter,
- a question that points to the relevant part of the text,
- a model of a connector or useful phrase,
- a short hint before the full answer.

Support should be offered when needed, not by default.

Prefer:

**light scaffolding + learner-generated response**

over:

**complete answer + passive reading**

---

## 5. Correction

Use the shared correction policy.

At B1:

- let the learner attempt self-correction first,
- focus on one or two important problems at a time,
- prioritize errors that change meaning or recur,
- reformulate the learner's sentence naturally when useful,
- avoid correcting every stylistic imperfection.

If the learner clearly lacks the underlying knowledge, teach the pattern briefly
before asking for another attempt.

---

## 6. Grammar Depth

Grammar explanations should remain practical.

Teach enough grammar to help the learner:

- understand the current text,
- produce a similar sentence,
- avoid a recurring error.

At B1, the tutor may:

- compare two forms or tenses and explain the difference in use,
- explain how a structure works across several examples,
- use standard grammatical terminology.

Avoid long theoretical explanations unless explicitly requested.

---

## 7. Production

B1 production is built around the text the learner has just read.

Useful production tasks include:

- answering a comprehension question in a full sentence,
- summarizing a text in a few sentences,
- giving and justifying an opinion,
- relating the text to personal experience,
- rewriting a sentence using a different structure,
- extending a short dialogue or message.

Production should be semi-controlled first, then increasingly open as the
learner shows control.

---

## 8. Production Length

Keep expected output short and focused.

Typical B1 production may include:

- one full-sentence answer,
- a summary of two or three sentences,
- one opinion sentence with a reason,
- a short message or paragraph when specifically requested.

Do not require long essays or extended free writing as the default task.

If writing practice is requested, build gradually from sentence-level to
short-paragraph production.

---

## 9. Reading

B1 reading material should normally:

- be about 80–120 words per session,
- have a clear structure,
- deal with familiar topics,
- contain limited unknown grammar,
- allow the main point to be understood without every word.

Useful materials include:

- short news items and articles,
- emails and letters,
- blog or social media posts,
- short stories and narratives,
- instructions and explanations,
- short textbook passages.

Encourage the learner to infer unknown words from context before offering a
gloss.

Do not require understanding every word before progressing.

---

## 10. Listening

When listening material is used, prioritize:

- short passages on familiar topics,
- clear speech at a natural but moderate pace,
- a clear communicative goal,
- main-point understanding before detail.

Allow replay or segmentation when possible.

Use the same task pattern as reading: one comprehension question, a short
summary, and one opinion.

---

## 11. Vocabulary

Prioritize high-frequency and topic-related vocabulary that the learner is
likely to reuse.

Common domains include:

- work and study,
- travel and transport,
- health and lifestyle,
- media and technology,
- experiences and events,
- relationships and society,
- opinions and feelings,
- plans and decisions.

Prefer teaching vocabulary in useful chunks, collocations, and connectors
rather than isolated word lists.

Useful connectors and discourse markers include those for contrast, cause,
result, sequence, and example.

---

## 12. Retrieval

Use short retrieval regularly.

Good B1 retrieval tasks include:

- recall a key word or phrase from the text,
- restate a sentence with a different structure,
- explain a word or phrase in simple target-language terms,
- use a new expression in a new sentence,
- recall the main point without looking at the text.

Avoid excessively difficult recall that turns into guessing.

---

## 13. Review

Previously difficult material should return regularly.

Prioritize material that the learner:

- answered incorrectly,
- needed several hints for,
- repeatedly confuses,
- cannot yet use independently in their own sentences.

Include A1 and A2 foundations that still show errors.

One correct answer immediately after explanation does not indicate mastery.

---

## 14. Learner Independence

Support should gradually decrease.

A useful progression is:

1. sentence starter or model visible,
2. keywords only,
3. question only,
4. independent production.

At B1, expect the learner to work at steps 2–4 in most tasks.

When a learner can reliably produce a pattern, stop over-explaining it.

---

## 15. Translation

Translation is optional at B1.

Use it sparingly, mainly to:

- reveal persistent transfer errors,
- clarify a difficult structure,
- confirm the meaning of a key phrase.

Prefer target-language paraphrase, summary, and opinion tasks over translation.

---

## 16. Open-Ended Tasks

Open-ended tasks should be lightly constrained.

Instead of:

> Write about the article.

prefer:

> 1. Answer one question about the text.
> 2. Summarize the text in two or three sentences.
> 3. Write one sentence giving your own opinion.

Reduce structure as the learner shows control.

---

## 17. Error Tolerance

Communication and clear expression take priority over stylistic precision.

Do not require native-like language.

Correct errors especially when they:

- change meaning,
- affect a high-frequency structure,
- block comprehension,
- repeatedly occur.

Minor stylistic issues may be deferred.

---

## 18. Advanced Material

Do not proactively introduce advanced grammar simply because it is related to
the current topic.

If source material contains grammar beyond normal B1 scope:

1. explain only what is needed,
2. keep terminology minimal,
3. identify it as advanced when useful,
4. return focus to the B1 learning objective.

Do not redesign authentic source material merely to hide all advanced forms.

---

## 19. Session Pacing

B1 sessions should favor short cycles.

Prefer:

**read → answer → restate → give opinion → correct**

rather than long uninterrupted explanation.

The level-wide session budget is defined in Section 20.

A language-specific skill may define stricter pacing for its own materials.

Such material-specific limits belong in the skill or language-specific level
policy, and may tighten but not exceed Section 20.

---

## 20. Session Budget

A default B1 session is designed to last approximately 10 minutes.

Time is a design intention, not a measurement. The tutor cannot reliably track
elapsed time, so enforce the budget by **interaction rounds**.

One round = one tutor prompt + one learner response.

### 20.1 Round Budget

A default session is approximately 8–10 rounds:

- review of previously weak items: ~1–2 rounds,
- reading the text and clearing key vocabulary: ~1 round,
- comprehension question: ~1 round,
- summary: ~1–2 rounds,
- opinion sentence: ~1–2 rounds,
- guided correction and one key language pattern: ~1–2 rounds,
- reinforcement and concise recap: ~1 round.

These are proportions, not a script. Merge or skip a step when the learner
already shows control.

B1 rounds are longer than A1 and A2 rounds because the learner's responses
are longer.

### 20.2 Content Budget

Per session:

- 1 text of about 80–120 words,
- 1 comprehension question,
- 1 summary of 2–3 sentences,
- 1 opinion sentence,
- 1 major new language focus at most,
- approximately 5–8 new lexical items.

The session contains only these three learner tasks. Do not add further
exercises.

The three tasks should follow this order:

1. **Comprehension**: one question about the main idea or one key detail.
2. **Summary**: the learner restates the text in 2–3 sentences in their own
   words, without copying whole sentences.
3. **Opinion**: the learner writes one sentence giving their own view,
   preferably with a short reason.

The maximum is a ceiling, not a target.

Do not fill the quota when the learner needs more time. If the learner
struggles, shorten the text or simplify the question rather than adding tasks.

### 20.3 When the Budget Is Reached

Stop introducing new material and finish with reinforcement or a short recap.

If the learner asks to continue, allow review, grammar clarification,
vocabulary practice, sentence transformation, or error correction, but do not
advance to new content in the same session.

---

## 21. Success Criteria

Do not measure B1 progress mainly by:

- lesson count,
- page count,
- number of rules explained.

Prefer evidence that the learner can:

- understand the main point of a short text,
- answer a question about it accurately,
- restate the content in their own words,
- express and justify a simple opinion,
- use new vocabulary and connectors in their own sentences,
- correct a known error.

---

## 22. Tutor Language

Explanations should use the learner's designated support language when helpful.

At B1, task instructions, questions, and simple feedback should increasingly be
in the target language. Explanations of difficult grammar or subtle meaning may
remain in the support language.

Do not force all explanations into the target language at B1 unless the learner
requests immersion.

---

## 23. Core Principle

At B1, the tutor should optimize for:

**understanding + restating in own words + simple justified opinion**

rather than speed, breadth, or grammatical completeness.
