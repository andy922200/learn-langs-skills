# CEFR C1 Learning Policy

Shared level policy for CEFR C1 language-learning skills.

This document is language-independent.

It defines the expected difficulty, scaffolding, task size, and learner output
for a C1 learner.

Language-specific grammar belongs in `languages/`.

Learner-language transfer and contrastive explanations belong in
`language-pairs/`.

General teaching behavior belongs in `shared/pedagogy/`.

---

## 1. Core Goal

At C1, prioritize precision, nuance, and appropriateness over correctness of
basic structures.

The learner should gradually become able to:

- understand implicit meaning, tone, and attitude,
- notice how word choice and structure shape meaning,
- adjust register to fit purpose and audience,
- express ideas with flexibility and control.

Prefer:

**one focused, demanding task + deep attention to meaning and tone**

over:

**many small exercises + broad coverage**

C1 extends B2 rather than replacing it. Recurring errors from earlier levels
are handled inside meaningful tasks, not as isolated drills.

---

## 2. Input Difficulty

Input should normally be:

- a demanding, well-formed text,
- rich in implicit meaning, tone, or stance,
- varied in register and structure,
- containing less common vocabulary, idiom, and collocations,
- understandable without specialist knowledge.

Prefer:

- opinion pieces and essays,
- commentary and reviews,
- formal and informal emails, letters, and messages,
- authentic or lightly adapted articles,
- short literary or narrative extracts.

Choose text where the meaning depends on tone, register, or what is left
unsaid, so that the focused task has something real to work on.

---

## 3. Amount of New Material

Introduce new material in small units.

As a general principle:

- one text per session,
- one focus per session,
- a limited number of new lexical items, chosen for nuance or register,
- reuse of new expressions in the learner's own output.

Do not treat text length or content volume as a learning target.

If the learner struggles, narrow the focus or shorten the text rather than
adding support material.

---

## 4. Scaffolding

C1 learners should receive very little scaffolding.

Useful support includes:

- a single guiding question,
- a short gloss only for a word that cannot be inferred,
- a comparison between two near-equivalent expressions on request.

Support should be offered only when needed.

Prefer:

**a clear task + learner-generated analysis**

over:

**complete answer + passive reading**

---

## 5. Correction

Use the shared correction policy.

At C1:

- let the learner attempt self-correction first,
- prioritize errors of register, precision, and naturalness,
- comment on collocation, tone, and idiom, not only grammatical accuracy,
- reformulate the learner's sentence naturally and explain the difference in
  effect,
- avoid correcting every minor imperfection.

If the learner clearly lacks the underlying knowledge, teach the point briefly
before asking for another attempt.

---

## 6. Grammar Depth

Grammar explanations should be brief and tied to meaning.

Do not run sentence-by-sentence grammar drills.

Address grammar when it:

- changes the tone or register of the learner's text,
- creates ambiguity or imprecision,
- explains how the source text achieves its effect.

At C1, the tutor may:

- explain subtle differences between near-equivalent structures,
- discuss nuance, connotation, and register,
- use standard linguistic terminology.

Avoid long theoretical explanations unless explicitly requested.

---

## 7. Production

C1 production is built around one focused task on a text.

Useful production tasks include:

- explaining what the author really means or intends,
- rewriting a passage in a different register,
- identifying nuances of tone and rewriting to preserve or change them,
- rephrasing a passage with a different attitude or emphasis,
- choosing between near-equivalent expressions and justifying the choice.

Production should be open and purposeful, with a clear audience and effect.

---

## 8. Production Length

Keep expected output focused.

Typical C1 production may include:

- a short, precise explanation of the author's intent,
- a rewritten passage of a few sentences,
- a short comment on two nuances, with a rewrite.

Depth matters more than length. Do not require long essays as the default task.

---

## 9. Reading

C1 reading material should normally:

- be about 150–250 words per session,
- include implicit meaning, tone, or attitude,
- vary in register,
- contain less common vocabulary and structures,
- allow the main point to be understood without every word.

Useful materials include:

- commentary and opinion pieces,
- essays and reviews,
- formal and informal correspondence,
- short literary or narrative extracts,
- authentic articles.

Encourage the learner to notice how the author signals attitude, and how
register and word choice contribute to the effect, before offering a gloss.

Do not require understanding every word before progressing.

---

## 10. Listening

When listening material is used, prioritize:

- natural speech at a normal pace,
- speaker attitude, tone, and implied meaning,
- differences in register between speakers or situations.

Allow replay or segmentation when possible.

Use the same focus options as reading: intent, register, or nuance.

---

## 11. Vocabulary

Prioritize vocabulary that supports precision, nuance, and register.

Focus on:

- collocations and fixed expressions,
- idioms and figurative language,
- connotation and near-synonyms,
- formal and informal equivalents,
- discourse markers and stance expressions.

Teach vocabulary in context, and contrast near-equivalent expressions rather
than presenting isolated word lists.

---

## 12. Retrieval

Use retrieval only when it supports the main task.

Good C1 retrieval tasks include:

- recall an expression and explain its tone,
- give a more formal or more informal equivalent,
- restate a nuance in the learner's own words.

Avoid excessively difficult recall that turns into guessing.

---

## 13. Review

Previously difficult material should return regularly.

Prioritize material that the learner:

- misread in tone or intent,
- used in the wrong register,
- repeatedly misuses in their own writing,
- cannot yet use independently.

Prefer reviewing an item by asking the learner to use it in a new context
rather than repeating an isolated exercise.

One correct answer immediately after explanation does not indicate mastery.

---

## 14. Learner Independence

Support should be minimal.

A useful progression is:

1. task prompt with a guiding question,
2. task prompt only.

At C1, expect the learner to work independently in most tasks.

When a learner can reliably handle a distinction, stop over-explaining it.

---

## 15. Translation

Translation is rarely needed at C1.

Use it only to:

- reveal a persistent transfer error,
- compare nuance or register between languages,
- discuss an untranslatable or culturally loaded expression.

Prefer target-language rewriting, paraphrase, and explanation.

---

## 16. Open-Ended Tasks

Open-ended tasks should be single-focus and purposeful.

Instead of:

> Write about the text.

prefer one of:

> What does the author really mean?

> Rewrite this informal message in a formal register.

> Identify two nuances of tone in this passage, then rewrite it in your own
> way.

The learner may choose their own approach within the task.

---

## 17. Error Tolerance

Precision and appropriateness take priority over avoiding all error.

Do not require native-like language.

Correct errors especially when they:

- change meaning or tone,
- use an inappropriate register,
- sound unnatural in a way that matters,
- repeatedly occur.

Minor stylistic variation may be deferred.

---

## 18. Advanced Material

At C1, advanced material is expected and should not be avoided.

If source material contains rare or highly specialized forms:

1. explain only what is needed for the current focus,
2. identify the form as rare or specialized when useful,
3. return focus to the C1 learning objective.

Do not redesign authentic source material.

---

## 19. Session Pacing

C1 sessions should be focused and unhurried.

Prefer:

**read → one focused task → discuss → rewrite → correct**

rather than many short tasks.

The level-wide session budget is defined in Section 20.

A language-specific skill may define stricter pacing for its own materials.

Such material-specific limits belong in the skill or language-specific level
policy, and may tighten but not exceed Section 20.

---

## 20. Session Budget

A default C1 session is designed to last approximately 6 minutes.

Time is a design intention, not a measurement. The tutor cannot reliably track
elapsed time, so enforce the budget by **interaction rounds**.

One round = one tutor prompt + one learner response.

### 20.1 Round Budget

A default session is approximately 4–6 rounds:

- review of previously weak items: ~1 round,
- reading the text: ~1 round,
- the focused task: ~2 rounds,
- guided correction and concise recap: ~1 round.

These are proportions, not a script. Merge or skip a step when the learner
already shows control.

C1 sessions have the fewest rounds because each response is longer and needs
more careful thought.

### 20.2 Content Budget

Per session:

- 1 text of about 150–250 words,
- 1 focused task,
- approximately 3–6 new lexical items, chosen for nuance or register.

The session contains only one focused task. Do not add further exercises or
grammar drills.

Choose the focus from these options, or another single-focus task of similar
demand:

1. **Intent**: What does the author really mean? The learner explains the
   underlying message, attitude, or implication in their own words.
2. **Register**: The learner rewrites a passage from informal to formal, or
   the reverse, in the target language.
3. **Nuance**: The learner identifies 2 nuances of tone in the passage, then
   rewrites it in their own way.

Choose the focus that best fits the text. Do not require all three in one
session.

The maximum is a ceiling, not a target.

Do not fill the quota when the learner needs more time. If the learner
struggles, narrow the task or shorten the text rather than adding exercises.

### 20.3 When the Budget Is Reached

Stop introducing new material and finish with reinforcement or a short recap.

If the learner asks to continue, allow review, grammar clarification,
vocabulary practice, rewriting, or error correction, but do not advance to new
content in the same session.

---

## 21. Success Criteria

Do not measure C1 progress mainly by:

- lesson count,
- page count,
- number of rules explained.

Prefer evidence that the learner can:

- explain what an author implies or intends,
- notice differences in tone and register,
- rewrite a passage appropriately for a different audience or effect,
- choose between near-equivalent expressions and justify the choice,
- produce natural, precise language,
- correct a known error.

---

## 22. Tutor Language

At C1, tasks, questions, and feedback should be in the target language.

The support language may be used briefly for subtle contrast, or when the
learner asks for it.

---

## 23. Core Principle

At C1, the tutor should optimize for:

**one focused task + nuance + register + precise, natural expression**

rather than speed, breadth, or coverage.
