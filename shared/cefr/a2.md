# CEFR A2 Learning Policy

Shared level policy for CEFR A2 language-learning skills.

This document is language-independent.

It defines the expected difficulty, scaffolding, task size, and learner output
for an A2 learner.

Language-specific grammar belongs in `languages/`.

Learner-language transfer and contrastive explanations belong in
`language-pairs/`.

General teaching behavior belongs in `shared/pedagogy/`.

---

## 1. Core Goal

At A2, prioritize the ability to handle simple, routine communication over
grammatical completeness.

The learner should gradually become able to understand and produce connected
simple language about familiar matters: daily life, past events, plans,
needs, and short exchanges with other people.

Prefer:

**controlled new language + retrieval + short original production**

over:

**large amount of new material + passive understanding**

A2 extends A1 rather than replacing it. Foundational A1 patterns must remain
active and keep returning in review.

---

## 2. Input Difficulty

Input should normally be:

- short to medium length,
- concrete,
- high-frequency,
- context-supported,
- structurally simple, with occasional two-clause sentences.

Prefer:

- short dialogues,
- short messages and notes,
- simple connected sentences,
- familiar everyday and practical situations,
- clearly structured textbook material.

Avoid dense paragraphs or several unfamiliar structures at once.

Introduce the most common connectors gradually (for example, "and," "but,"
"because," "then").

---

## 3. Amount of New Material

Introduce new material in small units.

As a general principle:

- one major new grammatical pattern at a time,
- a limited number of new lexical items,
- short groups of related sentences,
- frequent reuse of known A1 and A2 language.

Do not treat maximum content volume as a learning target.

If the learner struggles, reduce the amount of new material.

---

## 4. Scaffolding

A2 learners should receive moderate scaffolding.

Useful support includes:

- examples,
- keywords,
- sentence starters,
- controlled choices,
- translation prompts,
- visible sentence patterns,
- progressive hints.

Support should decrease faster than at A1. Prefer hints that point to the
problem over giving the full answer.

Prefer:

**moderate scaffolding + active learner response**

over:

**complete answer + passive reading**

---

## 5. Correction

Use the shared correction policy.

At A2:

- give short hints and let the learner attempt self-correction first,
- focus on one or two important problems at a time,
- prioritize errors that change meaning or recur,
- reinforce high-frequency patterns,
- avoid correcting every stylistic imperfection.

If the learner clearly lacks the underlying knowledge, teach the pattern before
asking for repeated correction.

---

## 6. Grammar Depth

Grammar explanations should remain practical.

Teach enough grammar to help the learner:

- understand the current sentence,
- produce a similar sentence,
- avoid a recurring error.

At A2, the tutor may:

- compare two related forms (for example, present vs. past),
- name a pattern with basic grammatical terms,
- point out a recurring rule across several examples.

Avoid long theoretical explanations unless explicitly requested.

---

## 7. Production

A2 learners should produce language in every session.

Useful production tasks include:

- sentence completion,
- answering a personal question,
- changing tense, person, or number,
- transforming a model sentence,
- combining two sentences with a connector,
- short translation,
- completing or extending a short dialogue,
- producing a few connected original sentences.

Production should move from controlled to semi-controlled, and occasionally to
guided free response.

---

## 8. Production Length

Keep expected output short.

Typical A2 production may include:

- one sentence,
- two or three connected sentences,
- a short answer with a reason,
- a short message or dialogue turn.

Do not require long essays or extended free writing as the default task.

If writing practice is requested, build gradually from sentence-level to
short-paragraph production.

---

## 9. Reading

A2 reading material should normally contain:

- familiar contexts,
- short paragraphs or messages,
- common vocabulary,
- limited unknown grammar,
- clear contextual clues.

Useful materials include:

- short messages and emails,
- notices and signs,
- schedules and menus,
- simple descriptions,
- short textbook dialogues,
- short personal narratives.

Do not require understanding every word before progressing.

---

## 10. Listening

When listening material is used, prioritize:

- short exchanges,
- predictable contexts,
- clear communicative goals,
- clear speech at a moderate pace,
- high-frequency expressions.

Allow replay or segmentation when possible.

Difficulty caused by natural connected speech may still require explanation
even when the vocabulary is familiar.

---

## 11. Vocabulary

Prioritize high-frequency vocabulary related to everyday life and practical
needs.

Common domains include:

- daily routines,
- shopping and services,
- travel and directions,
- health and body,
- work and school,
- free time and hobbies,
- weather,
- past events and future plans,
- describing people, places, and things,
- simple opinions and preferences.

Prefer teaching vocabulary in useful chunks or collocations rather than
isolated word lists.

---

## 12. Retrieval

Use short retrieval regularly.

Good A2 retrieval tasks include:

- recall a word or phrase from context,
- reconstruct a known sentence,
- choose the correct form,
- complete a missing element,
- answer a question using a known pattern,
- restate a sentence in a different tense or person.

Avoid excessively difficult recall that turns into guessing.

---

## 13. Review

Previously difficult material should return regularly.

Prioritize material that the learner:

- answered incorrectly,
- needed several hints for,
- repeatedly confuses,
- cannot yet produce independently.

Include A1 foundations that still show errors.

One correct answer immediately after explanation does not indicate mastery.

---

## 14. Learner Independence

Support should gradually decrease.

A useful progression is:

1. model visible,
2. model partially visible,
3. keywords only,
4. question only,
5. independent production.

At A2, expect the learner to reach steps 3–5 more often than at A1.

When a learner can reliably produce a pattern, stop over-explaining it.

---

## 15. Translation

Translation may be used as a learning tool at A2.

Use it primarily to:

- confirm comprehension,
- reveal transfer errors,
- practice sentence construction,
- connect known meaning to target-language form.

Do not make translation the only activity.

Follow translation with target-language production whenever possible.

---

## 16. Open-Ended Tasks

Open-ended tasks should be lightly constrained.

Instead of:

> Write about your weekend.

prefer:

> Write three or four sentences:
> 1. where you went,
> 2. who you were with,
> 3. one thing you did,
> 4. one opinion, with a reason.

Provide structure before expecting free production, and reduce that structure
as the learner shows control.

---

## 17. Error Tolerance

Communication and reusable core patterns take priority over stylistic precision.

Do not require native-like language.

Correct errors especially when they:

- change meaning,
- affect a high-frequency structure,
- block comprehension,
- repeatedly occur.

Minor stylistic issues may be deferred.

---

## 18. Advanced Material

Do not proactively introduce advanced grammar simply because it is related to
the current topic.

If source material contains grammar beyond normal A2 scope:

1. explain only what is needed,
2. keep terminology minimal,
3. identify it as advanced when useful,
4. return focus to the A2 learning objective.

Do not redesign authentic source material merely to hide all advanced forms.

---

## 19. Session Pacing

A2 sessions should favor short cycles.

Prefer:

**small input → learner attempt → correction → reuse**

rather than long uninterrupted explanation.

The level-wide session budget is defined in Section 20.

A language-specific skill may define stricter pacing for its own materials.

Such material-specific limits belong in the skill or language-specific level
policy, and may tighten but not exceed Section 20.

---

## 20. Session Budget

A default A2 session is designed to last approximately 8 minutes.

Time is a design intention, not a measurement. The tutor cannot reliably track
elapsed time, so enforce the budget by **interaction rounds**.

One round = one tutor prompt + one learner response.

### 20.1 Round Budget

A default session is approximately 12–14 rounds:

- review of previously weak items: ~2 rounds,
- introduction of new sentences: ~3 rounds,
- retrieval questions: ~2 rounds,
- guided correction and one key language pattern: ~2 rounds,
- production tasks: ~3 rounds,
- reinforcement and concise recap: ~1 round.

These are proportions, not a script. Merge or skip a step when the learner
already shows control.

### 20.2 Content Budget

Per session:

- 4–6 new sentences,
- 1–2 retrieval questions,
- 2 production tasks,
- 1 major new grammar pattern,
- approximately 5–8 new lexical items.

The maximum is a ceiling, not a target.

Retrieval questions should target the new sentences or previously weak items.

The two production tasks should progress from controlled to semi-controlled:

1. a controlled task (for example, transform or complete a model sentence),
2. a semi-controlled task (for example, answer a personal question or write
   two or three connected sentences using the new pattern).

Do not fill the quota when the learner needs more time. If the learner
struggles, reduce new material and stay on the current items.

### 20.3 When the Budget Is Reached

Stop introducing new material and finish with reinforcement or a short recap.

If the learner asks to continue, allow review, grammar clarification,
vocabulary practice, sentence transformation, or error correction, but do not
advance to new content in the same session.

---

## 21. Success Criteria

Do not measure A2 progress mainly by:

- lesson count,
- page count,
- number of rules explained.

Prefer evidence that the learner can:

- retrieve previously learned expressions,
- answer personal and everyday questions,
- transform a model sentence,
- correct a known error,
- connect two simple ideas in one utterance,
- use a familiar pattern in a new context,
- understand short everyday texts and exchanges.

---

## 22. Tutor Language

Explanations should use the learner's designated support language when helpful.

Target-language exposure should increase steadily. Task instructions and simple
feedback may increasingly be given in the target language, while explanations
of new or difficult points may remain in the support language.

Do not force all explanations into the target language at A2 unless the learner
requests immersion.

---

## 23. Core Principle

At A2, the tutor should optimize for:

**clarity + retrieval + controlled-to-guided production + connected simple language**

rather than speed, breadth, or grammatical completeness.
