# CEFR C2 Learning Policy

Shared level policy for CEFR C2 language-learning skills.

This document is language-independent.

It defines the expected difficulty, scaffolding, task size, and learner output
for a C2 learner.

Language-specific grammar belongs in `languages/`.

Learner-language transfer and contrastive explanations belong in
`language-pairs/`.

General teaching behavior belongs in `shared/pedagogy/`.

---

## 1. Core Goal

At C2, prioritize pragmatic sensitivity, stylistic control, and precise,
economical expression.

The learner should gradually become able to:

- understand implicit, ironic, and culturally loaded meaning,
- judge why one formulation is more natural or appropriate than another,
- shape tone, stance, and style deliberately,
- write concisely and precisely with a natural feel.

Prefer:

**one small piece of real material + one demanding refinement task**

over:

**large volume of input + broad coverage**

C2 extends C1 rather than replacing it. At this level, remaining errors are
usually subtle and should be handled inside meaningful tasks, not as isolated
drills.

---

## 2. Input Difficulty

Input should be authentic, and need not be simplified or adapted.

It should normally be:

- a short passage of real material,
- rich in implicit meaning, irony, or stylistic choice,
- varied in register, tone, and genre,
- containing idiom, cultural reference, or wordplay where relevant.

Prefer:

- news reports and commentary,
- opinion pieces and columns,
- essays and reviews,
- literary extracts,
- informal and formal correspondence,
- transcripts of natural speech.

Choose material where small differences in wording have a real effect, so that
the refinement task has something to work on.

---

## 3. Amount of New Material

Keep the material small and the analysis deep.

As a general principle:

- one short passage per session,
- one high-level task per session,
- new vocabulary only where it serves nuance, style, or register,
- reuse of new expressions in the learner's own output.

Do not treat text length or content volume as a learning target.

Depth of attention matters more than the amount of material.

---

## 4. Scaffolding

C2 learners should receive almost no scaffolding.

Useful support includes:

- a single task prompt,
- a comparison of two near-equivalent versions on request,
- a brief note on a cultural reference the learner cannot be expected to know.

Support should be offered only when needed.

Prefer:

**a clear task + independent learner judgment**

over:

**explanation before the learner has attempted the task**

---

## 5. Correction

Use the shared correction policy.

At C2:

- let the learner attempt self-correction first,
- focus on pragmatic fit, idiomaticity, concision, and style,
- explain why one option sounds more natural, not only that it does,
- offer an alternative formulation and compare the effect,
- accept multiple valid stylistic solutions,
- avoid correcting minor preferences that do not affect meaning or naturalness.

The learner's judgment should be engaged: ask them why they chose a form
before offering the tutor's view.

---

## 6. Grammar Depth

Grammar explanations should be brief, and tied to pragmatic or stylistic effect.

Do not run grammar drills.

Address grammar when it:

- explains a difference in naturalness or register,
- changes tone, stance, or emphasis,
- accounts for how the source text achieves its effect.

At C2, the tutor may:

- discuss subtle differences between near-equivalent constructions,
- discuss pragmatics, connotation, and idiomaticity,
- use linguistic terminology freely.

Avoid long theoretical explanations unless explicitly requested.

---

## 7. Production

C2 production is built around one high-level task on a small piece of real
material.

Useful production tasks include:

- rewriting a passage to keep the meaning but change the tone (for example,
  more ironic, more formal, more detached),
- explaining why one sentence is more natural, in pragmatic terms, than an
  alternative,
- rewriting a passage more precisely, concisely, and naturally,
- adapting a passage for a different audience or genre,
- comparing two versions and judging which fits a given context.

Production should be open and purposeful, with a clear audience and effect.

---

## 8. Production Length

Keep expected output focused.

Typical C2 production may include:

- a rewritten passage,
- a short pragmatic explanation of one or two choices,
- a concise, refined version of a paragraph, with brief justification.

Depth and precision matter more than length. Do not require long essays as the
default task.

---

## 9. Reading

C2 reading material should normally:

- be authentic and unsimplified,
- be about 200–300 words per session,
- involve implicit meaning, irony, or stylistic effect,
- include idiom, cultural reference, or wordplay where relevant,
- be understood without translating every word.

Useful materials include:

- news reports and commentary,
- opinion pieces and columns,
- literary extracts,
- essays and reviews,
- natural-speech transcripts.

Encourage the learner to notice how the author achieves tone and effect through
word choice, structure, and register before offering any explanation.

---

## 10. Listening

When listening material is used, prioritize:

- natural, unsimplified speech,
- irony, humor, understatement, and implied meaning,
- differences in register, accent, and speaking style.

Allow replay or segmentation when possible.

Use the same focus options as reading: tone, pragmatic naturalness, or precise
rewriting.

---

## 11. Vocabulary

Prioritize vocabulary that supports precision, style, and natural expression.

Focus on:

- idiom, collocation, and register-marked expressions,
- connotation and fine distinctions between near-synonyms,
- irony, understatement, and figurative language,
- culturally loaded or regional expressions,
- economical alternatives to wordy phrasing.

Teach vocabulary in context and by contrast, not as isolated word lists.

---

## 12. Retrieval

Use retrieval only when it supports the main task.

Good C2 retrieval tasks include:

- recall an expression from the passage and explain its pragmatic effect,
- produce a more concise or more natural equivalent,
- restate a nuance in the learner's own words.

Avoid excessively difficult recall that turns into guessing.

---

## 13. Review

Previously difficult material should return regularly.

Prioritize material that the learner:

- misjudged in tone, register, or naturalness,
- expressed in a wordy or unidiomatic way,
- repeatedly misuses in their own writing,
- cannot yet use independently.

Prefer reviewing an item by asking the learner to use it in a new context.

One correct answer immediately after explanation does not indicate mastery.

---

## 14. Learner Independence

Support should be near zero.

At C2, the learner is expected to work independently and to defend their
choices.

The tutor acts more as an editor and discussion partner than as an instructor.

When a learner can reliably handle a distinction, stop over-explaining it.

---

## 15. Translation

Translation is rarely needed at C2.

Use it only to:

- reveal a persistent transfer error,
- compare pragmatic or cultural nuance between languages,
- discuss an untranslatable or culturally loaded expression.

Prefer target-language rewriting, comparison, and discussion.

---

## 16. Open-Ended Tasks

Open-ended tasks should be single-focus, demanding, and purposeful.

Instead of:

> Write about the text.

prefer one of:

> Keep the meaning, but make the tone more ironic.

> Explain why this sentence sounds more natural than the alternative.

> Rewrite this paragraph more precisely and concisely, as a native writer
> would.

The learner chooses their own approach within the task.

---

## 17. Error Tolerance

Naturalness and precision are legitimate goals at C2.

Native-like expression may be a target for the rewriting task, but it is not a
requirement for every learner utterance.

Correct errors especially when they:

- change meaning, tone, or pragmatic effect,
- sound unnatural in a way a native reader would notice,
- are wordy or imprecise,
- repeatedly occur.

Accept valid stylistic variation. There is often more than one good solution.

---

## 18. Advanced Material

At C2, advanced and rare material is expected and should not be avoided.

If source material contains rare, regional, archaic, or highly specialized
forms:

1. explain only what is needed for the current task,
2. identify the form as rare or marked when useful,
3. return focus to the C2 learning objective.

Do not simplify or redesign authentic source material.

---

## 19. Session Pacing

C2 sessions should be focused and unhurried.

Prefer:

**read → one high-level task → discuss the choices → refine**

rather than many short tasks.

The level-wide session budget is defined in Section 20.

A language-specific skill may define stricter pacing for its own materials.

Such material-specific limits belong in the skill or language-specific level
policy, and may tighten but not exceed Section 20.

---

## 20. Session Budget

A default C2 session is designed to last approximately 6 minutes.

Time is a design intention, not a measurement. The tutor cannot reliably track
elapsed time, so enforce the budget by **interaction rounds**.

One round = one tutor prompt + one learner response.

### 20.1 Round Budget

A default session is approximately 4–6 rounds:

- review of previously weak items: ~1 round,
- reading the passage: ~1 round,
- the high-level task: ~2 rounds,
- discussion of choices and concise recap: ~1 round.

These are proportions, not a script. Merge or skip a step when the learner
already shows control.

C2 rounds are few and dense: each response is longer, and the tutor should ask
the learner to justify their choices.

### 20.2 Content Budget

Per session:

- 1 authentic passage of about 200–300 words,
- 1 high-level task,
- new vocabulary only where it serves nuance, style, or register.

The session contains only one high-level task. Do not add further exercises or
grammar drills.

Choose the task from these options, or another single-focus task of similar
demand:

1. **Tone shift**: keep the meaning, but change the tone (for example, more
   ironic, more formal, more detached).
2. **Pragmatic judgment**: explain why one sentence is more natural in context
   than another version.
3. **Precise rewrite**: rewrite a passage more precisely, concisely, and
   naturally.

Choose the task that best fits the passage. Do not require all three in one
session.

The maximum is a ceiling, not a target.

Do not fill the quota when the learner needs more time. If the learner
struggles, use a shorter passage or narrow the task rather than adding
exercises.

### 20.3 When the Budget Is Reached

Stop introducing new material and finish with a short recap.

If the learner asks to continue, allow review, discussion of alternatives,
further refinement of the same passage, or error correction, but do not advance
to new material in the same session.

---

## 21. Success Criteria

Do not measure C2 progress mainly by:

- lesson count,
- page count,
- amount of material covered.

Prefer evidence that the learner can:

- perceive irony, implication, and stylistic effect,
- explain why one formulation is pragmatically better than another,
- change tone while preserving meaning,
- write precisely, concisely, and naturally,
- defend stylistic choices,
- correct a known error.

---

## 22. Tutor Language

At C2, tasks, questions, and feedback should be in the target language.

The support language may be used briefly for cross-linguistic comparison, or
when the learner asks for it.

---

## 23. Core Principle

At C2, the tutor should optimize for:

**small authentic material + one high-level task + pragmatic judgment + precise, natural style**

rather than volume, breadth, or coverage.
