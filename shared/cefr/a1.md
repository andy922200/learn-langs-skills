# CEFR A1 Learning Policy

Shared level policy for CEFR A1 language-learning skills.

This document is language-independent.

It defines the expected difficulty, scaffolding, task size, and learner output
for an A1 learner.

Language-specific grammar belongs in `languages/`.

Learner-language transfer and contrastive explanations belong in
`language-pairs/`.

General teaching behavior belongs in `shared/pedagogy/`.

---

## 1. Core Goal

At A1, prioritize basic communicative ability over grammatical completeness.

The learner should gradually become able to understand and produce short,
high-frequency language related to immediate everyday needs.

Prefer:

**small amount of language + successful retrieval + reuse**

over:

**large amount of new material + passive understanding**

---

## 2. Input Difficulty

Input should normally be:

- short,
- concrete,
- high-frequency,
- context-supported,
- structurally simple.

Prefer:

- short dialogues,
- short sentences,
- simple questions,
- familiar everyday situations,
- clearly structured textbook material.

Avoid unnecessarily dense paragraphs or multiple unfamiliar structures at once.

---

## 3. Amount of New Material

Introduce new material in small units.

As a general principle:

- one major new grammatical pattern at a time,
- a small number of new lexical items,
- short groups of sentences,
- frequent opportunities to reuse known language.

Do not treat maximum content volume as a learning target.

If the learner struggles, reduce the amount of new material.

---

## 4. Scaffolding

A1 learners should receive relatively strong scaffolding.

Useful support includes:

- examples,
- keywords,
- partial sentences,
- controlled choices,
- translation prompts,
- visible sentence patterns,
- progressive hints.

However, support should not remove the need for learner retrieval.

Prefer:

**strong scaffolding + active learner response**

over:

**complete answer + passive reading**

---

## 5. Correction

Use the shared correction policy.

At A1:

- give clear and short hints,
- focus on one important problem at a time,
- avoid correcting every stylistic imperfection,
- reinforce high-frequency patterns,
- allow self-correction whenever realistic.

If the learner clearly lacks the underlying knowledge, teach the pattern before
asking for repeated correction.

---

## 6. Grammar Depth

Grammar explanations should remain practical.

Teach enough grammar to help the learner:

- understand the current sentence,
- produce a similar sentence,
- avoid a recurring error.

Avoid long theoretical explanations unless explicitly requested.

Prefer:

> This form changes because the subject is "I."

over a full morphological analysis when the simpler explanation is sufficient.

Technical terminology may be introduced gradually when it improves learning.

---

## 7. Production

A1 learners should produce language early.

Useful production tasks include:

- substitution,
- sentence completion,
- short translation,
- answering a simple question,
- changing person or number,
- transforming a model sentence,
- producing one short original sentence,
- completing a short dialogue.

Production should usually remain controlled before becoming open-ended.

---

## 8. Production Length

Keep expected output short.

Typical A1 production may include:

- one word,
- one phrase,
- one sentence,
- two or three connected simple sentences.

Do not require long essays or extended free writing as the default task.

If writing practice is requested, build gradually from sentence-level
production.

---

## 9. Reading

A1 reading material should normally contain:

- familiar contexts,
- short sentences,
- common vocabulary,
- limited unknown grammar,
- strong contextual clues.

Useful materials include:

- greetings,
- menus,
- timetables,
- short messages,
- simple descriptions,
- short textbook dialogues,
- basic forms,
- short personal introductions.

Do not require understanding every word before progressing.

---

## 10. Listening

When listening material is used, prioritize:

- short utterances,
- predictable contexts,
- clear communicative goals,
- repeated exposure,
- high-frequency expressions.

Allow replay or segmentation when possible.

At A1, difficulty caused by natural connected speech may require explanation
even when the vocabulary itself is basic.

---

## 11. Vocabulary

Prioritize high-frequency vocabulary related to immediate needs.

Common domains include:

- greetings,
- personal information,
- family,
- home,
- food and drink,
- numbers,
- dates,
- time,
- shopping,
- transportation,
- basic routines,
- common places,
- simple likes and dislikes.

Prefer teaching vocabulary in useful chunks or collocations rather than
isolated word lists.

---

## 12. Retrieval

Use frequent short retrieval.

Good A1 retrieval tasks include:

- recall a word,
- translate a short phrase,
- reconstruct a known sentence,
- choose the correct form,
- complete one missing element,
- answer using a known pattern.

Avoid excessively difficult recall that turns into guessing.

---

## 13. Review

Previously difficult material should return regularly.

At A1, foundational patterns deserve especially frequent review.

Prioritize material that the learner:

- answered incorrectly,
- needed several hints for,
- repeatedly confuses,
- cannot yet produce independently.

One correct answer immediately after explanation does not indicate mastery.

---

## 14. Learner Independence

Support should gradually decrease.

A useful progression is:

1. model visible,
2. model partially visible,
3. keywords only,
4. question only,
5. independent production.

When a learner can reliably produce a pattern, stop over-explaining it.

---

## 15. Translation

Translation may be used as a learning tool at A1.

Use it primarily to:

- confirm comprehension,
- reveal transfer errors,
- practice sentence construction,
- connect known meaning to target-language form.

Do not make translation the only activity.

Whenever possible, follow translation with target-language production.

---

## 16. Open-Ended Tasks

Open-ended tasks should be constrained.

Instead of:

> Write about your life.

prefer:

> Write three short sentences:
> 1. your name,
> 2. where you live,
> 3. one thing you like.

Provide structure before expecting free production.

---

## 17. Error Tolerance

Communication and reusable core patterns take priority over stylistic precision.

Do not require native-like language.

Correct errors especially when they:

- change meaning,
- affect a high-frequency structure,
- block comprehension,
- repeatedly occur.

Minor stylistic issues may be deferred.

---

## 18. Advanced Material

Do not proactively introduce advanced grammar simply because it is related to
the current topic.

If source material contains grammar beyond normal A1 scope:

1. explain only what is needed,
2. keep terminology minimal,
3. identify it as advanced when useful,
4. return focus to the A1 learning objective.

Do not redesign authentic source material merely to hide all advanced forms.

---

## 19. Session Pacing

A1 sessions should favor short cycles.

Prefer:

**small input → learner attempt → correction → reuse**

rather than long uninterrupted explanation.

The level-wide session budget is defined in Section 20.

A language-specific skill may define stricter pacing for its own materials, such
as a maximum number of textbook dialogue sentences per group.

Such material-specific limits belong in the skill or language-specific level
policy, and may tighten but not exceed Section 20.

---

## 20. Session Budget

A default A1 session is designed to last approximately 6 minutes.

Time is a design intention, not a measurement. The tutor cannot reliably track
elapsed time, so enforce the budget by **interaction rounds**.

One round = one tutor prompt + one learner response.

### Round Budget

A default session is approximately 10–12 rounds:

- review of previously weak items: ~2 rounds,
- introduction and retrieval of new sentences: ~2–3 rounds,
- guided correction and one key language pattern: ~2 rounds,
- short production tasks: ~2–3 rounds,
- reinforcement and concise recap: ~1 round.

These are proportions, not a script. Merge or skip a step when the learner
already shows control.

### Content Budget

Per session:

- default: 2 new sentences,
- maximum: 4 new sentences,
- 2 review items,
- 1 major new grammar pattern,
- approximately 3–5 new lexical items,
- 1–2 learner-production tasks.

The maximum is a ceiling, not a target.

Do not fill the quota when the learner needs more time. If the learner
struggles, reduce new material and stay on the current items.

### When the Budget Is Reached

Stop introducing new material and finish with reinforcement or a short recap.

If the learner asks to continue, allow review, grammar clarification,
vocabulary practice, sentence transformation, or error correction, but do not
advance to new content in the same session.

---

## 21. Success Criteria

Do not measure A1 progress mainly by:

- lesson count,
- page count,
- number of rules explained.

Prefer evidence that the learner can:

- retrieve a previously learned expression,
- answer a basic question,
- transform a model sentence,
- correct a known error,
- use a familiar pattern in a new simple context,
- understand short everyday input.

---

## 22. Tutor Language

Explanations should use the learner's designated support language when helpful.

Target-language exposure should increase gradually without making instructions
unnecessarily difficult.

Do not force all explanations into the target language at A1 unless the learner
requests immersion.

---

## 23. Core Principle

At A1, the tutor should optimize for:

**clarity + repetition + retrieval + controlled production + confidence in core patterns**

rather than speed, breadth, or grammatical completeness.
